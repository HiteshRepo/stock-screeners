# Recommendation Algorithm

The `divvy recommend` command uses a **weighted multi-criteria scoring model** to rank
watchlist candidates for a given investable amount. It is rule-based and fully
transparent — every score and its reasoning is printed in the output.

---

## What kind of algorithm is this?

It is a **rule-based recommender**, not a machine-learning model. Scores are computed
from live market data using fixed, human-interpretable rules. There is no training data,
no feedback loop, and no black box — you can see exactly why each stock ranked where it did.

---

## Scoring formula

Each candidate gets a total score between 0 and 1:

```
total_score = (yield_score × 0.40) + (div_score × 0.35) + (value_score × 0.25)
```

| Factor | Weight | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| `yield_score` | 40% | Trailing annual dividend yield |
| `div_score` | 35% | How much the stock diversifies the current portfolio |
| `value_score` | 25% | How far the price is below its 52-week high |

All three scores are normalised to `[0, 1]` across the candidate set before weighting,
so they are directly comparable regardless of unit.

---

## Factor breakdown

### yield_score (40%)

```
yield_score = candidate_yield / max_yield_in_candidate_set
```

- Source: `trailingAnnualDividendYield` from Yahoo Finance
- The highest-yielding candidate in the set always scores 1.0; others scale relative to it
- A candidate with 0% yield scores 0.0

### div_score — diversification (35%)

```
sector_pct  = sector's share of current portfolio cost basis (%)
div_score   = 1.0 - min(sector_pct / 100, 1.0)
```

- Measures how much of your existing portfolio is already in the candidate's sector
- A sector with **0% allocation** (not yet in your portfolio) scores **1.0**
- A sector that is **100% of your portfolio** scores **0.0**
- With an empty portfolio, every candidate scores 1.0 here — yield and entry point become the tiebreakers

### value_score — entry point (25%)

```
pct_below_52w_high = (52w_high - current_price) / 52w_high × 100
value_score        = pct_below_52w_high / max_pct_below_in_candidate_set
```

- Source: `fiftyTwoWeekHigh` from Yahoo Finance
- The candidate trading furthest below its 52-week high scores 1.0
- A stock trading at its 52-week high scores 0.0
- This is a rough proxy for valuation — a larger discount provides more margin of safety

---

## Pre-filtering

Before scoring, candidates are filtered out if:

- Yahoo Finance returned an error or no price (`is_valid == False`)
- The current price exceeds the investable budget (can't buy even 1 share)

## Rationale text

The "Why:" line printed under each recommendation is generated by `build_rationale()`
in `dividend/scoring.py`. It describes the top contributing factors in plain English:

| Condition | Text |
|---|---|
| yield ≥ 5% | "very high yield (X%)" |
| yield ≥ 3% | "solid yield (X%)" |
| yield ≥ 1.5% | "decent yield (X%)" |
| yield > 0% | "low yield (X%) — growth play" |
| yield = 0% | "no dividend data available" |
| sector not in portfolio | "X sector not yet in portfolio" |
| sector < 15% of portfolio | "X is underweight (Y% of cost basis)" |
| sector > 40% of portfolio | "⚠ X already Y% of portfolio — adds concentration" |
| ≥ 25% below 52w high | "deep discount entry (X% off 52w high)" |
| ≥ 12% below 52w high | "reasonable entry (X% off 52w high)" |
| < 3% below 52w high | "near 52w high — limited margin of safety" |

---

## Limitations

- **Weights are fixed.** The 40/35/25 split is hardcoded. A yield-focused investor and a
  value-focused investor would want different weights.
- **52w high is a noisy valuation proxy.** A stock can be 40% below its 52w high because
  it is genuinely cheap, or because its business is deteriorating.
- **No momentum signal.** The algorithm does not consider price trend, earnings growth,
  or analyst estimates.
- **Yield is trailing.** It reflects dividends paid in the past 12 months. A dividend cut
  already announced but not yet reflected will not be captured until Yahoo Finance updates.
- **No portfolio correlation.** Two candidates in different sectors may still be highly
  correlated (e.g. two PSU stocks).
